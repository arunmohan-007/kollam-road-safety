Black spot calculation

A 500 m stretch qualifies if it meets either MoRTH threshold using crashes from the three calendar years 2023–2025.

5 or moredistinct fatal or grievous-injury crashes
OR
10 or morepeople killed in road crashes

MoRTH defines the rule for National Highways. This study applies it to NH, SH and MDR, following NATPAC’s Kerala study. MoRTH black-spot circular

How Kollam black spots were identified

  1. Count each crash once. Group repeated rows by FIR and use the actual accident date. Use fatal and grievous-injury crashes for screening.
  2. Match crashes to the road network. Search up to 20 m on either side of the mapped road line and assign each matched point to one road part. Unmatched points stay visible in the collision layer and do not enter the screening.
  3. Move a 500 m window along the road. Measure distance along each continuous mapped road part. Test windows anchored at crash locations and the ends of the line, then count qualifying crashes and people killed within each window.
  4. Rank qualifying stretches. Use NATPAC’s Crash Severity Index. Where qualifying windows overlap on the same road part, keep the higher-scoring window first.

NATPAC CSI = 7 × fatal crashes + 3 × grievous-injury crashes

Data and methodology

Crash data is grouped by FIR and filtered by accident date. Casualties include workbook crashes with no usable map location when filters allow. Road-class filters use the GIS match. Black-spot cause summaries use the severe crashes assigned to each original 500 m stretch. Year, cause and crash severity filters show black spots with at least one matching contributing crash and keep the published three-year rank.

Road matching uses up to 20 m on either side of the mapped road. MoRTH qualifies a 500 m stretch with five or more fatal or grievous-injury crashes or ten or more people killed in three years. NATPAC CSI equals seven times fatal crashes plus three times grievous-injury crashes.
